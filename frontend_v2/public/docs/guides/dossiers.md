# Dossiers guide

A dossier gathers everything about one subject of the case in one place: a person, an organisation, a device, a vehicle, an address, an event or another subject. It holds your brief, the evidence and casework linked to the subject, images, assessments, interviews, and for a person, what the phones in the case hold about them.

Open **Dossiers** in the case sidebar. The page has two views: **Dossiers** and **Phone identities**.

## Find a dossier

The **Dossiers** view lists the subjects in the case, recently updated first.

- **Search subjects…** finds a dossier by name.
- The subject type filter narrows the list to people, organisations and the other types.
- The graph linkage filter shows dossiers that are **Graph linked**, **Unlinked** from the graph, or whose graph entity was deleted.

A dossier marked **Graph link needs review** or **Graph entity deleted** points at a graph entity that has changed. Open it and check its graph link before relying on its graph connections.

## Create a dossier

Someone who can edit the case sees **New Dossier**. The form asks for a **Working display name** and a **Subject type**, and offers a link to the subject's graph entity, **Why this subject matters**, an **Investigator summary** and **Case-specific roles** such as Witness, Source, Complainant or Custodian. A dossier can also be started from the **Add to Dossier** dialog in Evidence (see below).

## Read a dossier

A dossier opens in a panel with four tabs: **Brief**, **Media**, **Assessments** and **Interviews**.

- **Brief** holds the **Investigator brief**, your own written account of the subject, then **Collected work**: the evidence and casework linked to the dossier, each opening its source. It also shows contact details, **On the phones** for a person, accepted summaries and comparisons, and **Interviews at a glance**.
- **Media** holds the images chosen for the dossier, with captions and a cover image.
- **Assessments** holds investigator assessments, each with a category and the source it rests on.
- **Interviews** holds interviews and statements, with their date, participants, who interviewed and working notes.

**Add evidence** and **Add casework** under Collected work link more material. Linking adds to what is there; it never replaces another investigator's links.

## Add evidence to a dossier from Evidence

In Evidence, a file's menu in the file list, and the file's panel, offer **Add to Dossier**. The dialog **Add evidence to Dossier** lets you pick an **Existing Dossier** or create a new one by typing a working subject name. Press **Add to Dossier**.

- The file joins the dossier's collected work.
- A photo or other image is also added to the dossier's **Media**. When the dossier has no cover yet, the first image becomes its cover.
- An image already in the dossier's media is not added twice.

If the add stops part way, the message says that any links already made are kept, and that you can try again safely.

***When to use it.*** Use it while reading evidence: the file goes to the subject without leaving Evidence. A photo's own date, place and camera stay on the file in Evidence; they are the photo's data, not checked by a person.

## Add an image from the dossier

On the **Media** tab, **Add image** opens **Add evidence-backed image**. Choose an image already held in Evidence, add a caption that says why it matters, and tick **Use as Dossier cover** to make it the cover. The source file stays in Evidence unchanged.

Each image can take a caption, move earlier or later, become the cover with **Make cover**, or be removed from the dossier. Removing it does not delete the file from Evidence.

## Add from other screens

The **Add to…** menus on phone record lists, the Phone summary, Conversations, the People in contact panel on the Graph, the text workbench in Evidence and the search results page list the case's dossiers beside its theories, findings and notes. Choosing a dossier links the chosen records to it. Where a record cannot go into a dossier, the menu says why.

## What the phones hold about a person

A person's dossier has an **On the phones** panel. It lists each number, address and account linked to the person, and for each phone in the case:

- the first and last contact, or "No messages or calls";
- **Open conversations** with the count, which opens that person's messages and calls on that phone;
- the name each phone saves for them, with when the contact was created and changed, or "This phone holds no name for them."

From the panel, **Scope to these phones** opens Conversations on those phones, and **Compare phones** opens the phones side by side for this person. When nothing is linked yet, the panel offers **Review the suggestions** and **Link one in Phone identities**.

An identity can be unlinked from the panel. The panel shows what unlinking would change before you confirm it, and keeps a history of links, unlinks and rejections, with **Link again** or **Restore**.

## Phone identities

The **Phone identities** view lists every number, email address and account the phones in the case hold, busiest first. Each row shows:

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Identifier | The number, address or account, with its kind |
| Name on each phone | The name each phone saves for it, or "No phone gives a name" |
| Messages and calls per phone | The count on each phone; select one to open those conversations |
| First contact, Last contact | The first and last day of contact on any phone |
| Person | The dossier it is linked to, or "Not linked" |

Search by number, address, account or a phone's saved name, narrow to one phone, and show only identities **Linked to a person** or **Not linked**. The line above the table counts how many identities in the case are linked to a person.

***When to use it.*** Use it to find who stands behind a number, and to see which identifiers still have no person.

## Link an identity to a person

Select an identifier to open **Link to a person**. It lists the names each phone gives it and any **Suggested** people.

- **Choose** a suggestion, or pick a person from the list, or **A new person** with a name, then press **Link**.
- **Not the same person** rejects a suggestion. It will not be suggested again.
- **Unlink** removes a link and needs your reason.

Each link is saved under your name with a standard reason, shown before you save. A group chat or other group cannot be linked to a person. Only someone with the right to change links sees these controls; others see "A case admin can change links."

## Work through people suggestions

**People suggestions** opens the suggestions waiting in the case, one at a time. Each shows its strength, the names each phone saves, and its reasons, each with a count of records and up to three examples to open.

Choose **Link (y)**, **Not the same person (x)** or **Skip (s)**; **j** and **k** move to the next and previous suggestion. A suggestion with no dossier yet asks for a name for the new person. An optional note is saved with the link.

***Reading it.*** A suggestion is a lead from matching numbers, names and records, not a finding. The reasons and examples are there to check before you link. New suggestions appear when a phone is added or a dossier gains a contact detail.

## Put back what was removed

Assessments, interviews, images and evidence links removed from a dossier are kept. **Removed from this dossier** at the foot of the dossier lists each one, with when and by whom it was removed. Someone who can edit the case can press **Put back** to restore it as it was.

## Map positions of addresses

Under each address in a dossier's contacts, Loupe shows the address's map position, found through the case's address
lookup, as "Position ... Read by Loupe, not confirmed." Where there is no position yet, **Look up position** asks for
one. When none can be given, the line says why: the address service found no match, the words name no place on their
own, the case has no state set to bound the lookup, or the place is outside the case's state.

## Who overlaps with whom in Dossiers

Where the newer Corroborate is turned on, Dossiers has a third tab, **Who overlaps with whom**: every pair of people in
the case against every kind of overlap. The Corroborate guide explains how to read it.
