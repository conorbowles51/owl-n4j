# Statement automation baseline — 2 October 2026 (checkpoint A)

**Re-run after every release, from the repository root:**

```
LOUPE_BENCH_PYTHON=<python with backend + engine deps> scripts/run_statement_benchmark.sh [--out DIR]
```

The interpreter needs SQLAlchemy, pydantic, PyMuPDF, pytesseract and Pillow, plus the
`tesseract` binary. On the current host the application's own environment
(`<live tree>/.venv/bin/python`) has them and the script uses it read-only (no bytecode
writes). The run writes only to its run directory (default: a new directory under `$TMPDIR`),
prints the summary below, and leaves `results.json` (every period, reason, simulated action and
saved transaction check) next to it. Exit status 1 means at least one period was admitted that
should not have been, which is the case today (see Safety).

Measured on code `d3113874` (branch `fin/a-benchmark`, identical financial code to
`integration/evidence-main-reunion` at `687da296`), corpus `statement-automation-corpus-v1`.
Three consecutive runs produced identical statuses, reasons, simulated actions and saved
transactions.

## What limits automation most

1. **Merrick dates (4 of 4 Merrick periods blocked, 13 of 34 single-statement actions).** The
   reader recognises the printed statement (closing) date — the proposal exposes it as
   `printed_closing_date_iso` — but the review leaves both period bounds empty, so every period
   needs the end date entered and "start not printed" confirmed. The 29 September grouped
   decision collapses this to one action across the group, but nothing is automatic.
   Source-derived date provenance (plan §3) turns these three recoverable periods ready.
2. **Generic layouts have no quality-triggered image reread.** All three OCR defects in
   Credit One, Merrick and Andrews scans were repaired from the page image
   (`statement_page_reading: image_selected`); both damaged-digit cases in the generic layout
   stayed blocked as unreadable values, because `statement_reading_quality` only assesses those
   three families.
3. **Valid-but-wrong readings (the only safety failure).** A Credit One scan whose OCR layer
   reads 25.11 as 28.11 and 18.79 as 15.79 reconciles with every printed control and was
   admitted with two wrong amounts. Nothing triggers a reread when every value parses. The
   single valid-looking misread in a statement with running balances (generic-10) was caught
   by the balance check and held. Card layouts with only endpoint controls cannot catch
   compensating errors by arithmetic; this needs an independent source check (plan §3 step 4).
4. **Genuine decisions** (holder or account not printed, quiet periods) account for the rest.
   Quiet periods are held by policy ("equal balances alone do not establish no activity").

Holds and duplicates behave correctly: both omitted-row periods stayed out of the ledger and
the exact copy was held automatically at preparation.

## Method

- **Corpus** (`backend/benchmarks/statement_automation/corpus/`, generator `corpus.py`): 22
  synthetic PDFs, 27 statement periods (26 distinct plus one exact copy), all names and numbers
  invented. Pages are digital, "scanned with an OCR layer" (the image shows the true values and
  an invisible text layer carries the defect, the shape of a production scan with poor OCR) or
  image-only. `manifest.json` holds the ground truth: identity, period dates, opening/closing
  balances, every payment with date, amount, direction and printed running balance, and the
  expected disposition — `auto` (source establishes everything), `decision` (a fact a person
  must supply or a policy confirmation), `hold` (must not be admitted) or `duplicate`.
  Regeneration is byte-stable and the harness refuses a corpus whose hashes differ.
- **Pipeline exercised**: upload registration (`EvidenceDBStorage.add_files`); the evidence
  engine's `extract_text` (native text, table geometry, OCR fallback and quality-triggered image
  reread), `build_canonical_document_text` and `group_per_table_by_page` in a separate process;
  `import_batches.create_batch`, repeated `advance_batch` worker turns (catalog, proposal,
  readiness assessment, duplicate preparation), `batch_status`, `queue_import` with the displayed
  ready revision, and the strict writer via the worker. Not exercised: the engine job queue and
  HTTP hand-off, and the engine's async PostgreSQL write (the harness stores the same rows
  itself).
- **Database**: disposable SQLite per run. The loopback PostgreSQL on 55434 was not listening on
  this host, so audit triggers and row locking are not part of this measurement.
- **Ready without edits**: the batch offers the period for group import (`can_import`) with no
  saved review or edit of any kind. Denominator: distinct periods (exact copies counted
  separately).
- **Blocking reasons**: the batch's own classifier (`batch_review_summary.reason`) over each
  period's problems.
- **Human actions**: for each period that is not importable, the harness applies the ground
  truth exactly as an investigator would — each wrong or missing field, each decision ("start
  not printed", confirm no activity), each missing row — and re-runs the real `assess` to confirm
  the actions suffice. Counted: 1 to open the statement, 1 per edit or decision, 2 per row to add
  (values plus position). Held and duplicate periods count 2 (open + decide). The grouped figure
  counts holder, account, bank, currency and dates decisions once per family, matching the batch
  "Resolve together" actions. It is a lower bound: it assumes the investigator already knows the
  answer and ignores reading time and navigation between statements.
- **Critical fields**: every saved transaction is matched to the ground-truth period it was
  admitted from and compared for amount, direction, currency, account and date; missing and
  unexpected transactions are counted too.

## Timing (noisy host)

Engine reading took 77–108 s wall (182–264 s CPU including child processes) for 22 PDFs at
the engine's default concurrency of 2; batch preparation 2.3–2.6 s; group import 1.9–3.1 s;
total 85–118 s per run. A native page costs about 4–6 s, mostly worker process start-up; an
image reread costs 27–38 s per page and full-page OCR about 19 s. The host had 6 CPUs and a
1-minute load of 7–9 from other work during every run, so timings are indicative only and are
not comparable across hosts or loads.

## Limits and coverage gaps

- **Families not covered**: Capital One card, BBVA Mexico, Scotiabank Mexico, Monex, Kapital,
  Intercam and Santander Mexico statements, payment/receipt documents and deposit receipts. No
  Spanish-language or MXN material, no multi-currency statements.
- **Shapes not covered**: ruled (drawn-table) PDFs — every page here is read by text alignment;
  missing pages and continuations; internal repeated sections; legitimate equal-valued
  transactions; credit balances and closures with money owed; out-of-order pages; rotated or
  skewed scans; re-exported copies with different bytes (the copy here is byte-identical).
- **Synthetic scans are clean.** Pages are rendered at 200 dpi without noise, so OCR succeeds
  more easily than on real productions. Repair success here is an upper bound.
- **Small sample.** 26 distinct periods; one period moves a family percentage by 10–25 points.
  This corpus is the development set. Plan checkpoint C requires held-out documents that repair
  work has not seen.
- **Tracks**: fresh upload only. The existing-work and scale/interruption tracks are not built.
- The grouped-action figure assumes the 29 September grouped flows work as described. They
  were not exercised in a browser here.
