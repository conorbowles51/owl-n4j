# Case home guide

The case home is the first screen of every case. It gathers the team's work on the case: theories, findings, notes, tasks and deadlines, and what needs your attention. From here the side menu opens every other view of the case: evidence, graph, timeline, map, money and phones. Press Ctrl+K on any case screen to search the whole case.

## Main controls

The tabs across the top of the case home are:

- ***Overview.*** What needs your attention, recent casework, pinned evidence, the next deadlines and the case's dossiers.
- ***Casework.*** Every theory, finding and note in the case, with search, sorting and filters.
- ***Tasks & deadlines.*** Work assigned in the case and the dates it must meet.
- ***Case setup.*** The case summary, objective, constraints and key questions. People with view-only access see it as Case context.
- ***Agent proposals.*** Drafts the case assistant made when asked, waiting for a person to accept or reject them.

The bar at the very bottom of every case screen holds your recent steps and the Guide menu.

## Start here

***What it does.*** Start here is a card at the top right of the case home. It lists up to four first steps, each built from what the case holds today: upload files when there are none, process files not yet processed, open Financial when the ledger has payments, open Phones when the case has phones, open the graph once files are processed, and start a theory when the case has files but no theory. Each step has a button that goes straight to that screen.

***When to use it.*** On a new case, or on a case you have not worked on before, to see what is ready to look at.

***How to read it.*** A step only appears when its screen has something to show, so every button leads somewhere useful. The counts are true counts from the case. If something could not be counted, the card says so under the steps ("Not counted, could not be read: ..."). Hide the card with the cross at its top right; Guide brings it back with Show tips and tours again.

## The Overview tab

***What it does.*** Overview opens with the case name, its summary, its type and place, and the objective set in Case setup. Below that:

- ***Your work.*** Reminders and reviews assigned to you. Snooze a reminder for one day, or dismiss it until it changes. Dismissing a reminder hides it; it does not complete the work.
- ***Casework.*** The latest theories, findings and notes, with counts by type.
- ***Case right now.*** Important changes for the whole team, shown when there are any.
- ***Pinned evidence.*** Sources the team pinned, with a link to Evidence.
- ***Next up.*** The coming deadlines, with View all.
- ***Dossiers.*** The subjects at the centre of the case, and Interviews & statements when the case has them.

***When to use it.*** At the start of a working session, to see what changed and what is waiting for you.

## What changed

***What it does.*** What changed, at the foot of Overview, opens a brief of recent changes to the case, including how the money totals moved.

***When to use it.*** After time away from the case, or after a new production has been read in.

***How to read it.*** Money totals show before and after, by currency, for money in, money out and number of payments. When nothing was left unread, the brief says so.

## Casework

***What it does.*** Casework lists every theory, finding and note. Choose a type, search by words, sort by most recently updated, most recently created, title, significance or confidence, and filter by status, author or recent activity. Click an entry to open it beside the list. New casework, or New theory, New finding or New note when a type is chosen, starts a new entry.

***When to use it.*** To record what you observe as a note, a working explanation as a theory, and a conclusion the material supports as a finding. Linking each to its evidence keeps the reason for it beside it.

***How to read it.*** Each entry shows its type, its significance, its author and the items linked to it. A theory can be turned into a finding with Convert to finding. Adding items to a theory, and how each bears on it, is covered in the Theories guide.

## Compare what supports and contradicts a theory

***What it does.*** An open theory or finding with links shows "Compare what supports and contradicts it (N for, M against)". Open it to see the items marked Supports on one side and the items marked Contradicts on the other. Items linked as Context are counted below.

***When to use it.*** Before relying on a theory, to see the case for and against it side by side.

***How to read it.*** Each item names its kind, such as a payment or a document. Click one to open it. An empty side reads "Nothing is marked as supporting it" or "Nothing is marked as contradicting it": no one has linked an item that way, which is not the same as the evidence being absent.

## Links to review

***What it does.*** Links added before Loupe asked how an item bears on a theory appear under a heading such as "3 links were added without saying how they bear on this theory". Each has Supports, Contradicts and Context buttons.

***When to use it.*** When opening an older theory, to sort its links so they count in the comparison above.

***How to read it.*** These links stay exactly as they are until someone chooses. People with view-only access see the list without the buttons.

## Changed since cited

***What it does.*** When a payment cited by a theory, finding or note changes after it was cited, for example after a correction, the entry shows "Changed since cited" beside the link and a panel listing each change: the value cited and the value now. Open current payment goes to the payment as it stands.

***When to use it.*** Whenever you see the mark, before quoting the entry's figures.

***How to read it.*** The entry keeps showing the values it cited; they are not updated by themselves. Decide whether the entry still holds with the current value, and edit it if it does not.

## Restore deleted casework

***What it does.*** Delete, in an open entry, moves it to deleted casework and says it can be restored. Tick Include deleted in the Casework filters to list deleted entries, marked Deleted. Open one and click Restore to bring it back.

***When to use it.*** When an entry was deleted by mistake.

## Tasks and deadlines

***What it does.*** Tasks & deadlines lists the case's tasks and deadlines. A task has a title, description, priority, status (To Do, In Progress, Done), an assignee, an optional due date and time, an optional parent task and an optional linked deadline. A deadline has a name and a due date, and is marked complete when the work is finished. Filter by kind, status and case member.

***When to use it.*** To assign work to a teammate, or to track a date the case must meet.

***How to read it.*** A task linked to a deadline keeps its own status, so completing the deadline does not complete its tasks.

## Case setup

***What it does.*** Case setup holds the context and objective that guide the investigation: the case summary, the objective, constraints or special instructions, and key questions, one per line.

***When to use it.*** When the case opens, and whenever its scope changes. The objective appears at the top of Overview for everyone. If you leave with unsaved changes, Loupe asks before discarding them.

## Agent proposals

***What it does.*** Agent proposals lists drafts the case assistant made when asked: theories, findings, graph entities, statements and others. Filter by status, such as Accepted or Rejected.

***When to use it.*** After asking the assistant to draft casework, to check each draft against its sources.

***How to read it.*** None of these drafts is in the case until a person opens a cited source and accepts it. Each draft says what accepting it does, for example "Accepting adds it as a finding". A draft marked as a model reading stays a model reading until accepted.

## Your steps in the bottom bar

***What it does.*** The bar at the bottom of every case screen shows your last 8 steps in this case, oldest on the left, with the current one highlighted. A step is a screen you visited, with its filters and the item you had open. Click a step to return to exactly that view. The arrow at the right of the strip folds it to a single History button; click History to unfold it.

***When to use it.*** To get back to a view you were on a few screens ago without rebuilding its filters.

***How to read it.*** Only you see your steps. If a step's view cannot be rebuilt as it was, Loupe opens the nearest view and says so, for example when the item has since been deleted or merged. The message names the screen it opened instead.

## All your steps

***What it does.*** All steps, at the right of the strip, opens every step you took in the case, newest first, with a count. Search them by name, such as an account or a screen. Show more loads the next 50. Clear my history removes your steps for this case after you confirm.

***When to use it.*** To find a view from earlier in the week, or to retrace how you reached a finding.

***How to read it.*** Steps are kept for the number of days the dialog states (90 by default). Clearing your history does not touch trails already added to a theory.

## Add a run of steps to a theory

***What it does.*** In All steps, tick the steps that led to a finding, then click Add N steps to a theory. Shift-click ticks every step between two.

***When to use it.*** To keep the route that led to a conclusion with the theory, so a reviewer can follow the same path.

## Next steps cards

***What it does.*** When an action finishes, such as files processed, a statement imported, a report built or items deleted, a card appears at the bottom left with up to four next steps. Each step is one sentence with a button that goes there.

***When to use it.*** Straight after the action, to open its results.

***How to read it.*** A step only appears when its target has something, so a count of 0 never shows. The card stays until you use a step or dismiss it. With guidance off, or tips of that kind hidden, the card does not pop up and waits behind a Next steps link instead.

## The Guide menu

***What it does.*** Guide, at the right of the bottom bar, says whether guidance is on and offers:

- Take the tour of the screen you are on, and of an open panel that has its own tour.
- Start here: the case home tour.
- Read the guide for the screen or panel you are on, such as this one.
- Glossary of terms, the meaning of the words Loupe uses on its screens.
- Show tips and tours again, which brings back tips and Start here that were hidden.
- Turn guidance off, or Turn guidance on.
- Guidance settings, which opens your own settings, where hidden tips are listed by name.

When next-step cards are waiting, the menu counts them.

***When to use it.*** To take a tour again, to look up a word on screen, or to bring back a tip you hid.

## Tours

***What it does.*** The first time you open a screen with guidance on, a short tour points at its main controls, three to six steps. Next moves on, Skip tour ends it.

***How to read it.*** A step whose control is not on screen, such as a feature not yet used in the case, is skipped. A case owner or admin can set whether tours and tips are on by default for a case in Case settings; your own choice wins over it.

## Reports

***What it does.*** Reports, in the side menu, holds the case's saved financial reports and the casework report. New financial report, at the top right, opens Financial findings, where a financial report is assembled.

***When to use it.*** When the work needs to leave Loupe as a document.

## Casework report

***What it does.*** The casework report is a PDF of the case's findings, including financial findings saved from the ledger, with what each cites, then theories and notes. Tick the sections to include, move them up or down to set their order, choose a marking (No privilege marking, Confidential, or Privileged and confidential), then click Export casework report (PDF).

***When to use it.*** To share the state of the case with someone outside it, or to file it.

***How to read it.*** Each cited item shows whether it supports or contradicts, the reason it was added, who added it and when, and its production numbers. Model readings are marked as model readings. A theory adds its accepted counter-arguments and its timeline. The audit log is not included. The marking is your choice, not a legal decision about privilege.

## Financial reports

***What it does.*** Financial reports lists each saved financial report with its author and date. Each holds the selected financial findings with their note versions and supporting records. Create a report from financial findings opens Financial findings.

***How to read it.*** Reports show 25 to a page; Previous and Next move between pages.

## Case settings

***What it does.*** Case settings is the settings page of one case. Open in Case settings, beside a phone's outside-services choice on its Phone summary, goes to it. It holds:

- ***Case details.*** Title, description and status: Active, On hold or Closed. Save changes saves them for everyone.
- ***Address lookup.*** Automatic sends addresses found in the evidence to an outside address service, one a second, and places them on the map as not confirmed. Only when a person clicks keeps them in the map's Needs review list until someone clicks Look up or Look up all. The card says how many addresses are waiting.
- ***AI costs for this case.*** One line per document or conversation, with calls, dates and cost, for a chosen date range, and the case's spending limit. Download PDF saves it. Shown to the case owner and admins.
- ***Blur phone pictures.*** Whether pictures from phones are blurred in Media until opened, by default for this case. Each person can still turn blur off for themselves on the Media screen.
- ***Guidance.*** Whether tours and tips are on or off by default for this case. Shown to the case owner and admins.
- ***Phones.*** The time zone phone screens use, the default country for phone numbers, the tower reference used to place cell towers on the map, and which phones may be sent to outside services.
- ***Export the whole case.*** Builds one package of the complete case for another Loupe installation. Shown to the case owner.

***When to use it.*** When the case opens, and when its status changes.

***How to read it.*** People with view-only access can read the settings but not change them; the page says so. Changing the default country asks for a reason and reads those phones again.

## Export the whole case

***What it does.*** Build the package starts an export of the complete case. The card states what the package holds and what it leaves out. Each export is listed with its state: Waiting to start, Being built, Ready to download, or Removed after its keep time.

***When to use it.*** To move or copy a case to another Loupe installation.

***How to read it.*** Only one export runs at a time; the button reads "An export is running" until it finishes.

## Deleted cases

***What it does.*** Deleting a case from the case list moves it to Deleted items, after you type its title to confirm. The dialog first counts what the case holds. Deleted items, in the case list, lists the deleted cases you can restore, with who deleted each and when, what it holds and the days left. Restore brings a case back exactly as it was.

***When to use it.*** When a case was deleted by mistake, or to check what is waiting to be removed.

***How to read it.*** A deleted case disappears at once from every screen, search and the assistant. The case owner or an admin can restore it for the number of days shown (30 by default); after that it is removed for good. Days left shows in red from 3 days. If the delete dialog says the action is permanent, Deleted items is not in use on this installation, and the case cannot be restored. Deleted evidence files and graph items have their own lists, covered in the Evidence and Graph guides.
