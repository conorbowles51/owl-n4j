# Search guide

Case search looks through everything in one case at once: messages, calls, emails, people and identifiers, other phone records, documents and transcripts, machine-generated text, payments and production numbers. It finds records in two ways, by the words you type and by meaning, and shows both in one list.

## Open the search box

Press **Ctrl+K** (Cmd+K on a Mac) on any screen of a case, or use the search button in the case sidebar. Type a word, name, number or account. Pressing Ctrl+K again while the box is open selects what you typed, so a new search can start.

- Before you type, the box lists **Recent searches in this tab**.
- Letters written in fancy styles are found by their plain letters.
- Very short text is matched against numbers and identifiers only, and the box says so.
- **Exact characters** finds only the characters exactly as typed. It also turns off the search by meaning.

## Read the word results

Results come in groups, each with its full count, for example **Messages (214)** or **Payments (37)**. A group shows its first hits; press **Show all 214** to see every one. For messages, calls and emails, **Show all** opens Conversations with the same words, so the list there holds exactly what the count counted.

Under the groups, a footer says where nothing matched ("No matches in: Emails, Payments."), what each group searched, and what was not searched and why. When no record holds the words, the box says "No record holds these words as written:" with your words.

***Reading it.*** A count is every match in the case, not only the hits shown. A part of the case that was not searched is named in the footer; a group that is absent from the results is not the same as no match.

## Search by meaning

Below the word results, **By meaning** lists records that match what the words mean, even where the record uses other words. It covers phone messages, payments, accounts, judgement calls, saved money groups, statement pages, notes, findings and theories, the Timeline, people and names on the phones, transcripts and image text, documents, and the people, companies, places and connections in the graph.

The heading counts what is shown against all that was found: "By meaning (12 of 304, best first)". When everything found fits, it reads "By meaning (12)".

Each hit carries a line that says:

| Part | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Strong match, Moderate match, Weak match | How well a model judged the hit answers the search |
| The area | Where the record sits, for example Payments or Phone messages |
| by meaning, by words, by meaning and words | Which search found it |
| read by a model, not confirmed | The record's text was read from a page or image by a model, and no person has confirmed that reading |
| The title | The record's name |

Below that line sit the model's reason for the grade and a short extract from the record. Hits without a grade have not been graded by the model; the notes under the list say why, for example when ranking was not available.

Notes under the list say anything that limits the result, such as an area that could not be searched or a date window that narrowed it. **Not searched:** names what this search left out.

***When to use it.*** Use it when the exact words are unknown: a description of an event, a kind of payment, a topic in a conversation. Use word search for a name, number or account you have in front of you.

***Reading it.*** A grade and its reason are a model's judgement, not a finding. Open the record before relying on it. "Best first" means the list is ordered by grade, and the hits after the first page are still counted in the heading.

## When a search finds nothing

A search that finds nothing is a statement about what was searched, not proof that the case holds nothing. Before relying on it:

- Read the footer of the box. It names each part of the case that was not searched and why.
- Read any notice at the top of the results. A notice there says when a limit cut the search short.
- Turn **Exact characters** off, so the search by meaning runs too.
- Try another form of the name, number or account: with or without a country code, spaces or a middle name.
- Read the notes under **By meaning**. An area that could not be searched by meaning is named there.

## Open a hit

Select a hit, or move to it with the arrow keys and press Enter, to open its record: the message in its conversation, the payment, the document page, the graph item. Coming back to the search returns you to the same results at the same place in the list.

## Work with a full list of hits

**Show all** for a group other than messages, calls and emails opens the search page for that group, for example "Payments matching invoice". It counts every match, says which phones it covers, and lists every hit.

- Tick hits one by one, or tick **Every hit** to take the whole result.
- **Add to…** puts the chosen hits into a theory, finding, note or dossier. The add can be undone from the same place; the page says how many links the undo removed.
- **Corroborate** beside it takes the chosen hits into Corroborate (see the Corroborate guide).

Some hits have nothing that can be saved; the page counts them and leaves them out. Someone who cannot edit the case sees why the add is not available.

## Save a search as a keyword list

At the foot of the search box, **Save as keyword list** keeps the search as a named list of terms, one per line. With **Exact characters** on, the list matches exact characters too. Press **Save list**.

A saved list runs on every phone in the case within a minute, and again whenever a phone is added or read again.

## Read keyword lists

Open **Keyword lists** from the foot of the search box. Each list shows its terms, who saved it and a summary line of where it stands.

The table gives one row per term: the count across all phones, then a column per phone. Each phone's cell splits the count by kind of record; select a count to open those records. A **Total** row adds the counts up. A phone that could not be counted is named above the table with the reason.

**Attach to a theory** links the list to a theory you choose. The list then names every theory it is attached to.

***When to use it.*** Use a keyword list for terms that must be checked on every phone, including phones added later. The counts update by themselves when a phone is added or read again.

## What search by meaning reads

Search by meaning reads phone notifications as text, beside messages, calls and documents. A search that names a person
also reads the messages on that person's own phone as one more list. A snippet that cites a page shows the citation as
words, such as a file name and page. Graph names and summaries come after source records, so the record itself comes
first.
