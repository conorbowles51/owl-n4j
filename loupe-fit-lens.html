Market fit lens · 47 platforms · 30 axes

No platform fits every buyer. Change the market and the field reorders.

Forty-seven platforms scored on thirty axes — how each one models evidence, what it can do with that model, whether a team can run a case through it, what its AI layer is worth, and whether an institution can procure, deploy and rely on it. Pick a market and a budget to see what that buyer actually requires, who meets it, and what stands in the way.

The instrument exists because the search it describes actually happened: a team went looking for a platform to run live federal casework, tried several, and none could work the case. What follows is the field measured against that requirement.

Across the fifteen buyer profiles, not one platform of forty-seven can be sold into all of them, and only eight reach more than twelve. Loupe reaches thirteen, at an average fit of 75% — third of that group behind Magnet Axiom and Exterro FTK, and the only one of the three that models documents and financial records alongside device data. The two it does not reach are public defence, where competitors give the product away, and national security, which requires collecting evidence rather than receiving it.

Scores are judgements from published product documentation, release notes and press, banded 8–10 shipped and evidenced · 3–7 partial, gated or unverified · 0–2 no public evidence. Where a capability was searched for and not found, that is absence of evidence rather than proven absence. Price bands are entry-point estimates; several vendors publish nothing. Loupe's own row is scored on the same basis as every competitor, against the imminent release rather than today's build — bonded collections and the disconfirmation agent are both in that release. Competitors are likewise scored on shipped-and-announced capability.

Certifications are scored but excluded from market fit, for every platform equally. SOC 2, ISO 27001, CJIS and FedRAMP are procurement timing rather than capability — they are obtained when a market requires them, so gating on them measures how old a company is rather than how well its product fits the buyer.
